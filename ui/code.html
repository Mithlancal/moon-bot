Chemiosmotic Coupling & Proton-Motive Force

auto_awesome High-Yield Exam Core
Intuitive Mental Model

The chemiosmotic hypothesis describes how the energy derived from electron transfer through respiratory complexes (I, III, IV) is converted into chemical energy (ATP). Rather than forming a high-energy covalent chemical intermediate, the electron transport chain operates as a vector-oriented mechanical pump, expelling protons ($H^+$) from the mitochondrial matrix into the intermembrane space (IMS).

High-Yield Takeaways
01
Dual Electrochemical Components Proton-motive force ($\Delta p$) consists of an electrical membrane potential ($\Delta\psi \approx 160-180\text{mV}$) and a chemical proton gradient ($\Delta\text{pH} \approx 0.75-1.0\text{ units}$).
02
Asymmetric Coupling Topology Coupling requires an intact, proton-impermeable inner membrane; dissipating the gradient via ionophores (e.g., DNP, FCCP) uncouples respiration from phosphorylation without halting oxygen uptake.
calculate Fundamental Bioenergetics Equation Mitchell Proton-Motive Force ($298\text{ K}$)
Δp = Δψ - (2.303 RT / F) ΔpH
≈ Δψ - (59 mV) × ΔpH

Δψ: Membrane potential (N → P)
R & T: Gas constant & Kelvin temp
F: Faraday constant (96,485 C/mol)
